## Making Viral Research Go Viral
This guide provides concrete, actionable strategies for designing and promoting research that achieves maximum impact. Based on our successful experiences with DarkBench and other projects, these approaches will help your work reach key stakeholders and influence the AI safety landscape.

## **Part 1: Designing Research with Viral Potential**

### **Choose Topics at the Intersection of Technical and Social Impact**

**Example from DarkBench:** We identified "dark patterns" as a concept that existed in UI/UX but hadn't been systematically applied to LLMs. By connecting manipulative LLM behaviors to the familiar concept of dark patterns, we bridged technical AI evaluation with broader concerns about user manipulation.

**Concrete steps:**

1. Start a research document with two columns: "Technical aspects" and "Social implications"
2. Select the pairing with the strongest potential for both technical novelty and public interest
3. Draft a 1-paragraph research summary that explicitly connects the technical work to the social impact

### **Create Memorable Frameworks with SEO in Mind**

**Example from DarkBench:** We created the "DarkBench" name and website (darkbench.ai) specifically to rank for search terms like "LLM dark patterns," "AI manipulation," and "chatbot manipulation techniques." We made sure our framework had:

- A catchy, descriptive name that was easy to remember and search for
- Six distinct categories with clear names (e.g., "sycophancy" rather than just "agreement bias")
- Visuals that could be easily shared in presentations and media, available on the website

**Concrete steps:**

1. Research SEO keywords related to your topic using Google Trends and Keyword Planner
    - Identify high-volume search terms in your research area
    - Look for emerging terms with growing search volume
2. Create a name for your project/framework that incorporates key search terms
    - Keep it under 3 words if possible
    - Make sure domain name is available
3. If you have a taxonomy, keep it within 3-8 distinct categories (not too few, not too many)
4. Create a dedicated website with your project name in the URL
5. Use consistent wording across all social posts (DarkBench, AI Manipulation, LLM Dark Patterns etc.)

### **Quantify and Compare Socially Relevant Players with Clear Visuals**

**Example from DarkBench:** Since we showed how these capabilities changed between AGI companies, if people searched for OpenAI and sycophancy (as with the ChatGPT rollback), we would come up with concrete scores on a company they care about. The clear red-yellow-green visualization made it immediately obvious which models performed better or worse.

**Concrete steps:**

1. Try to include big names and if you have results that can be read as controversial, share them! (of course, never lie, but it's unethical to hide facts when they're true)
2. Ensure the scoring system is easy to understand (either take complex science and put colors on it or simply make the experiment easily understandable)
3. Create multiple visualizations showing the same data in different formats (which e.g. EpochAI does well):
    - Bar charts for direct comparisons across categories
    - Spider/radar charts to show the "profile" of each model
    - Summary table with overall scores and rankings
4. Make sure your charts work in both color and black-and-white (for print media)

## **Part 2: Amplifying Research After Completion**

### **Create Multiple Content Types for Different Audiences**

**Why it works:** Different stakeholders consume information in different formats.

**Action steps:**

- Academic paper for peer review and scholarly credibility
- Blog post with key findings in accessible language
- Twitter/X thread highlighting most surprising or concerning results
- One-page press release that you send to journalists that are linked with Apart
- (Maybe) Visual infographics illustrating key concepts and findings
- Video explainer, podcast episode or lecture for broader audience reach

### **Strategic Media Engagement with Concrete Examples**

**Example from DarkBench:** Our VentureBeat feature came after a journalist found our research when searching for "LLM dark patterns" following the ChatGPT-4o incident. We were prepared with clear talking points and striking visuals (see Talking with Journalists & Newspapers for tips), which made it easy for the journalist to craft a compelling story. We had a 40 minute interview where the questions had been sent beforehand.

**Concrete steps for journalist interactions:**

1. Prepare a media kit containing:
    - 5-7 pre-written quotes that can be directly used in articles
    - 3-5 high-resolution images/charts with clear captions
    - 1-page background document on your research
    - Short bio for each key researcher
2. Develop these specific talking points:
    - "The headline finding" (one sentence)
    - "The surprising discovery" (something counterintuitive)
    - "The concerning implication" (why people should care)
    - "The call to action" (what should change)
3. Practice delivery of these points in mock interviews
4. Record all interviews (with permission) for your reference
5. Follow up with written clarifications if anything was unclear

**Concrete steps for proactive outreach:**

1. For opinion articles:
    - Identify 3-5 publications that match your target audience
    - Research their specific submission guidelines
    - Draft a submission specifically for each outlet (not one-size-fits-all)
    - Use AI tools to help refine your writing for the publication's style
    - Include your affiliations and a 2-3 sentence bio highlighting expertise
2. For press releases:
    - Create a journalist contact database with:
        - Name, publication, and email
        - Previous AI coverage topics
        - Preferred contact method
    - Send personalized pitches to 10-15 most relevant journalists
    - Include a compelling subject line with your key finding
    - Keep the pitch under 150 words with a link to the full press release
    - Follow up once after 2-3 days if no response

### **Optimize Language for Search and Sharing**

**Example from DarkBench:** We deliberately used language that potential readers would search for. Instead of just using technical terms like "anthropomorphization" or "sycophancy," we included phrases like:

- "AI systems pretending to be human"
- "chatbots that agree with everything you say"
- "AI manipulation techniques"
- "deceptive AI practices"

**Concrete steps:**

1. Identify search terms for three distinct audiences:
    - Technical researchers (e.g., "LLM evaluation frameworks")
    - Industry practitioners (e.g., "AI safety testing methods")
    - General public/media (e.g., "Is my AI assistant manipulating me")
2. Create a document with alternative phrasings for key concepts:
    - Technical term: "Anthropomorphization"
    - Industry phrasing: "False persona creation"
    - Public phrasing: "AI pretending to be human"
    - Policy phrasing: “Consumer manipulation”
3. Use these alternative phrasings in:
    - Social media posts
    - Blog headlines and subheadings
    - Press release titles
    - Video/podcast descriptions
4. Test headlines with tools like CoSchedule's Headline Analyzer
5. Track which phrasings get the most engagement and refine accordingly

## **Conclusion**

Creating viral research in AI safety requires intentionality at every stage—from concept development through execution and communication. By choosing topics with broad relevance, creating clear frameworks, and strategically amplifying findings, researchers can ensure their work reaches the audiences necessary to drive positive change in AI development and governance.

Remember that viral reach is a means to an end—the ultimate goal is creating meaningful impact that leads to safer, more beneficial AI systems.
